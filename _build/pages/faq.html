Mortgage FAQs

The questions we hear most

Every situation is different, so treat these as a starting point. The first conversation with us is free.

How much can I borrow?
Most lenders start at around 4 to 4.5 times your income, and some go higher for certain professions or incomes. They also look at your outgoings, credit history, deposit and term. Try our calculators, then we'll check real lender criteria for you.
What is a decision in principle?
It's a lender's indication of how much it would be willing to lend, based on a soft look at your details. Estate agents often ask for one before you make an offer. It isn't a guarantee of a mortgage.
How do I find the best rate?
Rates change often. The lowest rate isn't always the cheapest deal once fees, deal type and term are included. We compare the total cost over the deal period, not just the headline rate.
I'm self-employed. Can I still get a mortgage?
Yes. Lenders use your tax calculations (SA302s) and accounts, and some will consider one year's figures, retained profits or contractor day rates. More on self-employed mortgages.
When should I start looking at remortgaging?
About six months before your current deal ends. Check your dates with our countdown.
Do you charge a fee?
Your first consultation is free. We typically charge £295 for arranging a mortgage, and never more than £1,000 or 3% of the loan, whichever is lower. See our fees.
Can we meet in person?
Yes. We meet face-to-face at Grove House, Chineham Court, or by video or phone, whichever suits you.
Do you arrange buy-to-let mortgages?
Yes, along with commercial and specialist lending and mortgages for foreign nationals. Some buy-to-let mortgages are not regulated by the FCA.
Who regulates MDJ Mortgages?
MDJ Mortgages Ltd is an appointed representative of Stonebridge Mortgage Solutions Ltd, which is authorised and regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority. Our FCA reference is 949077.
How do I make a complaint?
Contact Stonebridge on 0345 646 5505 or at complaints@stonebridgegroup.co.uk. If you're not happy with the outcome, you can refer your complaint to the Financial Ombudsman Service.
